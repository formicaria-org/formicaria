formicaria

A notebook for research and daily life that runs on your own computer: notes, tasks, a food log and a training log, kept as plain files you own.

Each note is one ordinary text file, in a folder you choose. You can read the files in any editor, copy them to a USB stick, or open them in ten years on a computer that has never heard of this app. There is no account and no subscription, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

It opens in your web browser, but it is not a website: the whole thing runs on your machine, and it works with the internet switched off.

This is a young project by one author, used daily on Linux and Android only. Builds for macOS, Windows and iPhone exist, but the author has not been able to check them. How far it is proven says exactly what that means.

Get it

Download the archive for your system from the latest release, unpack it, and double-click the launcher. Your browser opens by itself.

  • Linux

    Unpack the linux-x86_64 archive and double-click Start formicaria.sh.

    64-bit x86, glibc 2.34 or later (Ubuntu 22.04+, Debian 12+, Fedora 35+).

    • Download for Linux
    • Setting up on Linux

  • macOS

    Unpack the macos-arm64 archive and double-click Start formicaria.command.

    Apple silicon. The first time, macOS warns about unsigned software: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

    • Download for macOS
    • Setting up on macOS

  • Windows

    Unpack the windows-x86_64 zip and double-click Start formicaria.vbs.

    Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. The setting-up chapter says what to do about the security warning.

    • Download for Windows
    • Setting up on Windows

  • Android and iPhone

    The same release carries an Android .apk and an iPhone .ipa. Both need a little more than a double-click, and each has its own chapter.

    The iPhone chapter is not optional reading: Apple's free signing expires after seven days.

    • On an Android phone
    • On an iPhone

All you need besides is a current web browser — Firefox, Chrome, Safari or Edge. Closing the tab stops the app about a minute and a half later, unless a reminder is waiting. Later versions are installed from inside the app: Settings, Check now. Each archive also carries the whole manual, to read offline.

What you can do

  • Notes

    Write in Markdown, one note per file. The editor shows the result as you write, and the text is still the file. Search looks inside every note. A Logseq or Obsidian folder can be brought in, with its links kept.

    • Notes
    • Importing

  • Tasks and a calendar

    A task is a note with a date on it. Give a note a status or a date and the same notes become a board, or a month and week calendar.

    • Views

  • Reminders

    A note with a start or a due date reminds you at its time, once you turn that on in Settings.

    • Reminders

  • A food log

    Log what you eat from a list of 16,000 foods that comes with the app and is searched offline. Keep recipes, plan a menu with a shopping list, and follow a diet from a library of sourced ones or set your own targets.

    • Food and training

  • A training log

    A timetable for the week, 117 exercise cards in the words of the body that published each, and training regimes to follow, each with a week to start from.

    • Fitness

  • Backup, and working with someone else

    Send a notebook somewhere else to keep it safe, and see how a note looked last week. Two people can keep the same notebook in step, each on their own machine; if both edit the same note, their changes are merged rather than making you choose.

    • Backup
    • Collaboration

  • An assistant on your own device

    Optional, and off until you turn it on. A small AI model that runs on your own device answers in a note's discussion, can look something up on the web, and drafts edits for you to approve. No account and no key; your notes never leave the machine.

    • The study assistant

Food and fitness give no advice: every figure names the body that published it. They can be hidden in Settings if you want only a notebook. History and backup need the program git on Linux and macOS (Windows carries its own), and the assistant downloads its model the first time you turn it on. When something optional is missing, the app says so and nothing else is affected.

How far it is proven

Taken from the project's own status notes. What is listed above is what has been built, which is not the same as what has been proven on your machine.

  • Young. The work began on 14 July 2026. This is one person's project, built alongside their research rather than as a product.
  • Used daily on Linux and Android — the two systems the author works on, and the only two anything is checked against.
  • macOS, Windows and iPhone have been run by other people and reported working — the macOS report alongside real faults — but on early versions (0.2 and 0.3), not the current one. Nobody is known to have downloaded anything newer. None of it is verified by the author, who has no Mac, no Windows machine and no iPhone. The builds are made and tested automatically and the downloads are real; that is a different claim from someone having opened one.
  • The newest parts are the least proven. Food and fitness are early and have been used by the author alone. Reminders have never been seen to arrive on a phone, and their notifications on Windows and macOS have never been run.
  • The assistant has been run on Linux and Android. On macOS and Windows it is built but has never been run.
