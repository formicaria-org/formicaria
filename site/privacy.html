Privacy

formicaria runs on your own computer or phone. The project runs no server, has no accounts, and receives nothing from you — not your notes, not your mail, not how you use the app. Your notes are plain files in a folder you choose.

Gmail

If you connect Gmail, you do it with your own Google Cloud client, which you create yourself, and the app talks to Google directly from your computer.

  • Read only. The one permission asked for is gmail.readonly. Google itself refuses anything else made with it: the app cannot send, delete or change your mail.
  • Only the mail you choose. Only conversations carrying the Gmail label you chose, since the date you chose, are read — never the whole mailbox.
  • Where it goes. Each conversation becomes a note in the notebook you chose, on your computer, where the app's assistant (a model running on the same computer) reads it to find meetings. Nothing is sent to the project or to anyone else. If you copy that notebook online yourself — for example as a backup to GitHub — the notes go there too, as all your notes do.
  • The sign-in is kept in a file only your user account can read, on your computer. Disconnect in Settings deletes it and asks Google to cancel it.

formicaria's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other things that reach the internet — only when you set them up or ask

  • Backup sends your notes to the place you set up, such as your own GitHub repository.
  • A calendar address you add is fetched from your computer to place its meetings in your notes.
  • Food look-up asks Open Food Facts for one product, by its barcode, when you press for it.
  • The assistant's web search, if you turn it on, sends what it looks up to the search services the manual names.
  • Updates: the app asks the project's release page on GitHub whether a newer version exists. This can be switched off in Settings.

Questions

Ask in the project's issues. The source is open, so each of these statements can be checked against it.
