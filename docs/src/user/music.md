# Music

formicaria keeps your songs beside your notes. **A song is a note**, like a meal in Food: its title,
artist, album and the rest are the note's details, and its audio file is attached to it like any
other file. Open a song's note and it plays there.

> **New.** Built in November 2026 and tried on a computer only. You can add songs, browse them by
> song, album, artist and playlist, make playlists and play them.

## Turning it on and off

**Music** is one of the app's [groups](./views.md#the-groups), beside Food, Fitness and Health. It
opens on its player, with your songs under it. It is shown unless you hide it, with the same switch as the others:

**Settings → Food & reminders → Food and training → Show Food, Fitness, Health, Music and Videos on this device.**

## Adding a song

Press the **＋** in Music, then **Choose files** — one song or many — or drop the files onto the
sheet on a computer.

- **Each song fills itself in from its own file.** Most music files carry their details inside them:
  the title, artist, album, track number, year, genre, length and often the cover. formicaria reads
  them on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **A file with no details** is read by its name: `05 - Some Artist - Some Title.mp3` gives the
  artist *Some Artist* and the title *Some Title*.
- **One file** shows its form: change anything before you press **Save**.
- **Several files** show one line each. Press a line to change it, then **Save** them all at once.
- **A song you already have is not added twice.** If a file's audio is already one of your songs,
  its line says so and it is left out.

What you type is kept in the song's note. **The file itself is never changed.** The details its
file carried are kept on the file's own note too, so you can always see what it said.

Undo (**↶**) takes back the songs you just saved (a very large batch, 200 songs at a time).

## The player

The top of Music is a music player: a lit display above a row of keys. It is there before anything
plays; press a song below to start.

- **The display** shows the song scrolling past, its number in what is playing (*TRK 02/12*), the
  time — press it to see the time left instead — and how far along it is: press or drag the bar to
  jump. Lights show **SHUF**, **REP** (or **REP1**), **EQ** and the volume, and on a computer a level
  meter moves with the sound.
- **The keys**: shuffle, ⏮ (the start of the song, or the one before when you are near its start),
  ▶/❚❚, ⏭, and repeat — press it again for *all*, *one*, *off*. Where there is room (a computer, a
  tablet) also back and forward ten seconds and ■ stop.
- **Under the keys**: mute and the volume; **Sound**; **Queue** (what plays next: press one to play
  it, × to take it out); the **look**; and the song's **note** (on a narrow phone, the note is in
  the song's ⋯ instead).
- **Three looks**, changed in turn with the palette button: **Classic LCD** (the green display),
  **8-bit** (bricks, a blue sky and a little note running along as the song plays) and **Arcade**
  (a night stage: the artist and the song face each other across the clock, the song's bar wears
  down as it plays, the other bar is the volume). Only the look changes; every key is where it was.
- **Sound**, on a computer: a five-band equaliser with presets (*Flat*, *Bass boost*, *Rock*, *Pop*,
  *Jazz*, *Classical*, *Vocal*, *Treble boost*) or your own setting, and the balance between left and
  right. Not on a phone yet.
- **Keys on a computer**, while Music is open and you are not typing: **Space** play or pause,
  **← →** five seconds back or on, **N** next, **P** previous, **M** mute, **S** shuffle, **R**
  repeat, **+ −** volume. Your keyboard's media keys work too, anywhere in the app.

**It keeps playing wherever you go in the app.** Leave Music, or scroll the player out of sight,
and a slim bar appears at the bottom with the song and ⏮ ⏯ ⏭; press the song's name to come back to
the player (elsewhere in the app it opens **Now playing**: the cover, the controls and the queue).

**What is queued, and your volume, shuffle, repeat and sound, are kept on this device**, and come
back the next time you open the app, paused.

On a phone the music keeps playing when you leave the app or the screen turns off, with its keys on the lock screen, in the notification, on headphones, Bluetooth and in the car.

## Finding your music

Under the player: **Songs · Albums · Artists · Playlists**, and one search for all four. Type any
part of a title, an artist, an album or a genre; every word must match, and capitals and accents
don't matter (*beyonce* finds *Beyoncé*).

- **Press a song to play it.** The list you pressed it in becomes what plays next, in that order —
  search first to play just what you found. **Play** and **Shuffle** above a list play all of it.
- **Albums** are their covers; press one for its songs in track order.
- **Artists** list everyone with their albums and songs; a compilation's songs are under each of the
  artists who made them.
- **⋯ beside a song** (or beside an album's, artist's or the whole list's **Shuffle**): **Play next**,
  **Add to queue**, **Add to playlist…**, **Open its note**.

## Playlists

A playlist is a note tagged `playlist`, with its songs as a numbered list of links. You can open it
and edit it like any note — the list's order is the order it plays in — but you rarely need to:

- **Make one** with **New playlist** in Playlists, with **Save the queue as a playlist**, or from any
  ⋯ → **Add to playlist…** → a name → **Make it**.
- **Add to one** from any ⋯ → **Add to playlist…** → its name. A whole album or artist goes in at
  once from its own ⋯.
- **In a playlist**, a song's ⋯ also has **Move up**, **Move down** and **Take out of this
  playlist**. Words you wrote in the playlist's note around the list are kept.

Undo (**↶**) takes back any change to a playlist.

### Audio in your notes

A note can hold audio of its own — a recording, or tracks saved with Save media *into the note*. A
track saved into a note is also a song in your library, and the note keeps it as an attachment: one
file, two places to find it. Notes that hold audio are listed under the playlists as **In your
notes**: **Play** plays a note's tracks as they are, and **Add to playlist…** makes them songs — the
same files, nothing copied — then asks which playlist, as for any song. So a playlist can take songs
from anywhere: Music, a saved page, a note.

## Where your songs are kept

A song's file is kept in your notebook like any picture or PDF you attach, so it is in your
[backups](./backup.md) with everything else.

**Song files are not sent with your notes** to your other devices, unless the file is under your
notebook's *Send attachments under* limit **and** *Music and recordings travel with the notes too* is
ticked — off unless you tick it (see [Backup](./backup.md)). The songs' notes do travel, so
another device lists your songs; add the files there too to play them.

A file is copied when you add it: songs you also keep in a music folder take their space twice.

## From the web, and to other players

- **Songs from a web page**: *Save media* keeps a page's sound (or an embedded video's) as a song,
  into the playlist you choose. [Saving media](./media.md).
- **Other players**: each playlist is also written as a `.m3u8` file in the vault's `playlists/`
  folder on a computer, and any Subsonic player can stream your Music from it (Settings →
  Connections). The files are the vault's own; nothing is copied.
- **Videos** have a section of their own, built the same way: every video, collections, a search
  and a player.
