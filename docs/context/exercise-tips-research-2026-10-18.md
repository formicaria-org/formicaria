## The question

The owner: *"exercises that often lead to injuries, like with tools in a gym"*, then: *"find
reputable sources to add to each exercise's comments and tips, and eventually links to
multimedia content to help doing the exercise right, what to avoid to prevent injuries"*.

## The short answer

- **Where gym injuries come from is well documented, and mostly not technique.** In U.S.
  emergency departments, **65.5% of weight-training injuries were a weight dropped on the
  person** and 90.4% involved free weights (Kerr, Collins and Comstock, *AJSM* 2010; 25,335
  cases; *full text*, rechecked). Overexertion strains of the trunk come next. Rates per hour
  are low beside team sports (Keogh and Winwood, *Sports Med* 2017). Novices are at higher risk
  than experienced people (Kemler, *BMJ OSEM* 2022).
- **Text we may copy is generic, and on gym lifts it repeats.** Across the 46 cards that use a
  bar, free weights or a machine, 74 quotes are only **24 different sentences**. The top four are
  NIA's "don't hold your breath", "don't jerk or thrust weights", "warm up with less weight" and
  "don't lock your joints" — 44 of the 74. **No source we may copy covers the deadlift, the
  bent-over row, the lat pull-down, the leg press, the shrug or the push-press.**
- **Advice specific to each lift exists, but only as a link.** The ACE Exercise Library has a
  page for 78 of the cards. Its terms forbid copying ("No portion of…", *source*).
- **Films:** 22 of the 39 cards that had none now have one, almost all from a health or
  professional body (NHS inform, the National Institute on Aging, the Army's H2F channel,
  hospitals). 20 cards found nothing at all, listed below.

## Where the injuries come from — the evidence

All *abstract* unless noted; the ones marked ✓ were reopened by the checker and confirmed.

| Finding | Paper |
|---|---|
| ✓ 65.5% weight dropped on the person; free weights 90.4%; sprain or strain 46.1%; trunk most often hurt (upper 25.3%, lower 19.7%); people 55 and over more often hurt on machines (18.2% vs 9.3%) | Kerr, Collins, Comstock, *AJSM* 2010 (NEISS; *full text*) |
| ✓ Over 10 years: trunk the most injured site (40.5% of men, 31.1% of women); women more often hurt by dropped equipment (18.5% vs 12.1%), men more by pressing (7.2% vs 3.3%); "gym environments were the most common injury sites" | Coffey et al., *J Strength Cond Res* 2026 |
| ✓ Arm and shoulder: shoulder 33.8%, finger 28.1%; drop or crush 30.1%; people 65 and over twice the odds of pushing-movement injuries | Lee et al., *Orthop J Sports Med* 2026 |
| ✓ Fitness centres: overexertion 36.2% of all injuries and 52.6% of free-weight injuries; "crush injuries due to falling weights were common for all free weight activities" | Gray and Finch, *Inj Epidemiol* 2015 |
| ✓ Dutch gyms: 73.1% while unsupervised; bench press 11.8% and squats 9.6% of exercise-specific injuries; overuse or overload the top cause | Kemler, Noteboom, van Beijsterveldt, *BMJ OSEM* 2022 (CC BY-NC) |
| Novices at higher risk every year; most injuries still in experienced people, since there are more of them | Kemler, Valkenberg, Verhagen, *BMJ OSEM* 2022 |
| Training at home went with fewer injuries than in a gym (case-control) | Noteboom et al., *JSAMS Plus* 2023 |
| 0.24–1 injuries per 1,000 h in bodybuilding; weightlifting 2.4–3.3, powerlifting 1.0–4.4 (competitive lifters) | Keogh and Winwood 2017; Aasa et al. 2017; Tung et al. 2024 |
| ✓ Bench press alone: a bar on the neck killed 2 local lifters and "at least 11" in national data 1999–2002 | Jumbelic, *J Forensic Sci* 2007 |
| ✓ Behind-the-neck press and pull-down ("high-five" position) go with signs of shoulder instability — an association | Kolber, Corrao, Hanney, *J Strength Cond Res* 2013 |
| ✓ A rounded back while lifting did not predict back pain — low-quality evidence, light lifts at work; **it says nothing about heavy deadlifts either way** | Saraceni et al., *JOSPT* 2020 |
| "Ego lifting" was not measured by any study; the phrase appears only in news coverage of a craniofacial-injury paper | Mangal et al. 2024 (*search*) |

**So the riskiest things, in order:** losing control of the weight (dropping, crushing,
pinching); overloading the trunk; bench press and other presses, above all alone without
safety bars; overhead and behind-the-neck shoulder positions; squats. Machines are not
risk-free: older people are over-represented on them.

**General rules with a source behind them:** add 2–10% only once the current load goes 1–2
repetitions past the target (ACSM 2009, *abstract*); lift free weights with a spotter or others
present, and check collars (Kerr 2010, copyrighted — link only); get shown how to set up and lift
before starting, machines included (Kerr 2010); supervise children; and NIA's warm up, breathe
out on the effort, don't lock joints, stop when it hurts (**quotable**).

## What this means for the cards — options for the owner, none decided

1. **A "Lifting safely" card, once, instead of the same four NIA lines on every gym card.** NIA's
   words, quoted and credited, shown from every equipment card. This spares 46 cards from
   repeating themselves.
2. **Quotes specific to a lift, on the cards that have them.** These come from NIA's booklet and
   *Growing Stronger*: "always bend forward from the hips, not the waist" on rows and the
   straight-leg deadlift; "keep your wrists straight" on curls and presses; "don't let momentum
   do the work" on step-ups. *Growing Stronger* needs its licence settled first.
3. **ACE links** on 78 cards ("how to do it, common mistakes"), marked as a link to a
   copyrighted page.
4. **The injury facts themselves cannot be quoted** — they are journal text — and a sentence like
   "don't bench alone without safety bars" would be **the app's own words**, which the
   2026-10-12 ruling does not allow on a card. Two ways: keep cards to quotes and links, and put
   the evidence in the manual with its citations; or a new ruling that lets a card carry one
   short, cited caution in the app's words, limited to the five risks above. **That is the
   owner's call** and would be a `decisions.md` entry either way.
5. **The 22 new films** fill cards that had none; follow the owner's order (body → confirmed
   expert → trainer → Army).
