# What the trackers may claim, and on whose authority — a source register (2026-10-11)

> **A first pass, and a contract.** The ruling is `decisions.md#data`, 2026-10-11, *nothing is
> invented*. This file is the list that ruling points at: every diet label, target, formula and
> training suggestion the app offers must name a row here. A row that has not been read at its
> source is marked, and **must be read before anything is built on it**.

## The owner's rule

> *"We need not to invent anything but base it on grounded and documented and well reputable
> sources after a proper research."* — on diet labels (Mediterranean, vegetarian, vegan), recipes,
> training regimes and workouts, and any suggestion computed from a person's height, weight or
> other measurements.

## How to read the *Checked* column

- **source** — the source's own page or document was fetched and read on 2026-10-11.
- **search** — taken from a search result or a secondary summary. Not yet read at the source.
- **memory** — from recall. Not looked up. Treat as a lead, not a fact.

Nothing marked *search* or *memory* may be shipped as a number, a definition or a claim.

the person fills in or is measured against, with its source shown.

**Computing a recipe's values.** Built 2026-10-11 as a plain sum of ingredients by weight,
divided by the servings a person gives; nothing is adjusted for cooking. The published method for
doing more — yield (weight change) and nutrient retention factors, as in EuroFIR's recipe

**"Recipes a person likes"** is the person's own data: their logged meals and saved foods. Nothing
is learned about taste from anywhere else.

### Following a regime — what a reputable source actually gives (2026-10-12)

Asked by the owner: diets to follow and training regimes of different kinds (swimming, pilates,
gym) "with known suggested routines (how are they determined…?)". What was read:

| Source | What it establishes | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| [WHO 2020 guidelines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/), adults 18–64 | "at least 150–300 min of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity, or at least 75–150 min of vigorous-intensity", or a mix, a week; "muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week"; more than 300 / 150 min for additional benefit. **Intensity**: moderate is 3 to under 6 METs, or "a 5 or 6" on a 0–10 scale of perceived exertion; vigorous is 6 METs or more, or "a 7 or 8". | [NHS, physical activity guidelines for adults 19 to 64](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/) | Which activities count as what: swimming and running as vigorous; brisk walking, water aerobics, cycling as moderate; **yoga, pilates, lifting weights, body-weight exercises as muscle-strengthening**. | Pilates trials in chronic low back pain (a 2025 synthesis; a 296-person dose trial) | Twice a week was generally more effective than once. Evidence for one condition, not a general prescription | search |
| A swimming or pilates **routine** published by an international or public-health body | **None found.** | — |

**What this means.** A reputable source gives a **dose** — minutes a week at an intensity, days
of strengthening, sets per muscle group — and says any activity of that kind counts. None gives a
session-by-session routine for swimming or pilates, and WHO says outright that the evidence does
not separate types. So "follow a known regime" can be grounded as *a weekly dose, by its source,
that the person fills with the activities they choose* — and not as a prescribed routine, which
would be ours.

**What a person would have to tell the app, and for what** (each already has a source row in §5):
sex, age, height, weight and an activity level for an energy estimate (EFSA: Henry × 1.4–2.0);
weight for protein per kilogram; nothing at all for WHO's activity dose, which is the same for
every adult 18–64 and is measured by perceived exertion, not heart rate. Age matters once: at 65
the recommendation changes. **Not for the app to assess**: pregnancy, chronic conditions and
disability, for which WHO has separate recommendations, and pre-activity screening (PAR-Q+ may
not be modified or used commercially).

**What cannot be built on today's data.** A dose in minutes needs an entry that records an
activity, its minutes and its effort; today a training entry is a set (reps and load). A diet
defined by servings of food groups (Mediterranean, DASH, Nordic) needs every food classified into
those groups; only Ciqual's foods carry a group. WHO's limit on *free* sugars cannot be checked:
the tables give total sugars.

**Named programmes** (branded routines, "5×5", couch-to-5k) are not sources. A programme the app
offers is built from the parameters above, and says which.

**The definitions, verbatim** (agreement in brackets):
- *Fasting*: "a voluntary abstinence from some or all foods or foods and beverages for preventive,
  therapeutic, religious, cultural, or other reasons" (85%).
- *Intermittent fasting*: "repetitive fasting periods lasting up to 48 h each. IF includes fasting
  regimens of 1 fasting day per week, 2 separate or consecutive fasting days per week,
  alternate-day fasting (ADF), and time-restricted eating (TRE)" (87%).
- *Time-restricted eating*: "food intake and the consumption of caloric beverages is restricted to
  a specific period of time during the day, resulting in a daily fasting window of at least 14
  hours. There is no explicit limit on energy intake during eating hours" (88%).
- *Alternate-day fasting*: "alternating a day of eating ad libitum and a day of water-only
  fasting" (94%). *Alternate-day modified fasting*: the same with "a day of modified fasting" (96%).
- *Modified fasting*: "limiting energy intake to typically up to 25% of energy needs on modified
  fasting days"; examples include "fasting practiced on 2 separate or consecutive days per week"
  (86%) — which is what is sold as "5:2", a name the table does not use.
- *Short-term fasting*: 2–3 days. *Prolonged fasting*: 4 or more consecutive days. *Periodic
  fasting*: any regimen "repeated at regular intervals".
- *Religious fasting*: "any fasting regimen that is undertaken as part of a religious practice";
  the comment names Ramadan and Bahá'í fasting as intermittent dry fasting, Buddhist practice as
  time-restricted eating, and the Christian Orthodox traditions and the Daniel Fast as diets
  restricting certain foods. *Intermittent dry fasting*: "most commonly… 9 to 20 h".
- *Therapeutic fasting* is "individually tailored to a person's age, sex, body composition,
  physical activity level, occupation, goal, and planned duration"; *medically supervised
  fasting* is applied "by a trained physician or similar credentialed healthcare provider".

**What a person could follow in the app, and what it would take.** Time-restricted eating: the
hours of the first and the last thing eaten each day — **entries are dated by day and carry no
time** (2026-10-11), so this needs either a time on an entry or two times on the day. Two fasting
days a week, or alternate days: the day's energy against 25% of the person's energy need, which
needs the profile. Fasts of two days or more, dry fasting and anything "therapeutic" are, by the
paper's own words, individually tailored or supervised: **not for an app to set**.

**Other practices, not yet looked at** (recalled, unchecked): gluten-free has an international
food standard (Codex); low-FODMAP has clinical guidance for one condition; ketogenic diets are a
medical therapy for epilepsy; calorie restriction is defined in the same consensus table. Halal
and kosher are rules about foods the tables do not describe.

**Not one number:** thiamin 0.1 mg and niacin 1.6 mg NE **per MJ of energy**; zinc 9.4–16.3 /
7.5–12.7 mg by how much phytate is eaten (upper level 25). **Sodium** is not in the 2017 report;
EFSA's 2019 opinion was not read, so the sodium limit shipped stays WHO's. Several upper levels do
not apply to what is naturally in food, and say so.

Labels print "% NRV" against other numbers: [Regulation (EU) 1169/2011, Annex XIII](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annex/XIII/adopted)
(read as adopted, at legislation.gov.uk; EUR-Lex would not open) — e.g. vitamin C 80 mg, calcium
800 mg, iron 14 mg, vitamin D 5 µg. A "significant amount" is 15% of those per 100 g.
[Regulation (EC) 1924/2006, Annex](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2006/1924/annex/adopted)
gives the conditions for a *claim*: "source of fibre" at least 3 g per 100 g, "high fibre" 6 g;
"source of protein" 12% of energy, "high" 20%; "source of" a vitamin or mineral a significant
amount, "high" twice that. They are rules for what a seller may say, not a definition of a food
rich in something; using them to label a food in the app would be a choice, and named as one.

### Meals and the time between them

| Source | What it establishes | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| [Gill & Panda 2015, *Cell Metab* 22(5):789](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4635036/) | An eating event is "calorie containing (≥5 kcal)"; events "with <15 minutes inter-event interval were combined into one 'meal'"; the daily eating duration is the interval holding 95% of all events **over the days monitored**, not one day's first to last | source |
| [Leech et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4501369/) (already above) | No standard: occasions split at 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes; some require 50 kcal | source |
| [Koppold et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11504329/) (already above) | Time-restricted eating: "a daily fasting window of at least 14 hours"; three experts "would have preferred … 'at least 12 h'" | source |
| A number of meals a day, or a time to eat, recommended by a public body | **None found** in WHO's fact sheet or the NHS's tips; the 2020 US advisory committee: "Insufficient evidence … Grade Not Assignable". Germany, France and Singapore not checked | source / source\* |

**So:** with a time on each entry, the hours from the last entry of one day to the first of the
next can be measured and held against the 14 hours of the definition. How many times a person ate
is a count under a stated rule (entries less than 15 minutes apart are one occasion — Gill and
Panda's), shown as what happened and **never against a number to reach**: nobody gives one.

### What the food tables can and cannot say about a food's group

- **Ciqual**: three official levels are in the files held (138 groups: meat by species, juices
  apart from fruit, plant-based alternatives apart from what they replace). Only the first is
  carried today. **USDA**: 172 *What We Eat in America* categories for survey foods and 28 food
  categories for Foundation foods, both in the files held; neither is carried.
- **BLS 4.0** publishes no key to its letters. Its site states a few (B bread; E eggs and pasta;
  G vegetables; U beef, veal, pork, mutton, lamb; X and Y dishes); the rest could only be paired
  with names by their order on a page, which is an inference.
- **No table tells whole grain from refined**, olive oil from other oils, or low-fat dairy from
  full-fat. **A mixed dish's group names its main ingredient**: 29% of BLS is dishes.
- FoodEx2 (EFSA) would answer most of it; neither table publishes its foods' codes.
