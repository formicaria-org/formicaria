# What the trackers may claim, and on whose authority — a source register (2026-10-11)

> **A first pass, and a contract.** The ruling is `decisions.md#data`, 2026-10-11, *nothing is
> invented*. This file is the list that ruling points at: every diet label, target, formula and
> training suggestion the app offers must name a row here. A row that has not been read at its
> source is marked, and **must be read before anything is built on it**.

## The owner's rule

> *"We need not to invent anything but base it on grounded and documented and well reputable
> sources after a proper research."* — on diet labels (Mediterranean, vegetarian, vegan), recipes,
> training regimes and workouts, and any suggestion computed from a person's height, weight or
> other measurements.

## How to read the *Checked* column

- **source** — the source's own page or document was fetched and read on 2026-10-11.
- **search** — taken from a search result or a secondary summary. Not yet read at the source.
- **memory** — from recall. Not looked up. Treat as a lead, not a fact.

Nothing marked *search* or *memory* may be shipped as a number, a definition or a claim.

**Consequence for the design.** A diet label cannot be a tag on a person that changes numbers
silently. It is either (a) a set of *excluded food groups*, checkable only if foods carry a
food-group classification the list does not yet have, or (b) a *named screener or serving table*
the person fills in or is measured against, with its source shown.

## 4. Recipes

A list of ingredients is a fact and is not protected; the *wording* of a recipe usually is. So a
recipe collection needs a licence for its text, not only for its idea.

| Source | Licence | Notes | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Wikibooks Cookbook](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook) | CC BY-SA 4.0 and GFDL (attribution, share-alike) | Community-written; quality uneven; a scraped copy exists on Hugging Face under the same licence | search |
| NHLBI *Keep the Beat* cookbooks | Published by NIH; **copyright status not established** — "Keep the Beat" is a trademark of HHS, which does not answer it | Heart-healthy recipes with nutrition analysis | search |
| USDA MyPlate Kitchen | Federal work | Reported retired with MyPlate.gov on 7 Jan 2026; not confirmed from an official page | search |
| RecipeNLG, Recipe1M+ | Non-commercial or by request | Not usable in a shipped app | memory |

**Computing a recipe's values.** Built 2026-10-11 as a plain sum of ingredients by weight,
divided by the servings a person gives; nothing is adjusted for cooking. The published method for
doing more — yield (weight change) and nutrient retention factors, as in EuroFIR's recipe
calculation procedure and USDA's *Table of Nutrient Retention Factors* — was **not read**, so none
of it is applied.

**"Recipes a person likes"** is the person's own data: their logged meals and saved foods. Nothing
is learned about taste from anywhere else.

**The words the training screens use**, held against the literature: *set*, *repetition*, *load*
and *session* are the field's ordinary terms. The total shown is **volume load** — "broadly
defined as the product of repetitions and amount of weight lifted", repetitions × external load
(kg), summed over exercises ([Schoenfeld et al. 2016, *Asian J Sports Med* 7(2)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003312/),
read at source), where *volume* alone is "commonly defined as the total number of repetitions".
It was labelled "Volume", which names the other thing; relabelled 2026-10-11. The app has **no**
muscle groups, exercise categories, training goals or effort scale, so there is none to ground;
an exercise's name is whatever the person types.

**Named programmes** (branded routines, "5×5", couch-to-5k) are not sources. A programme the app
offers is built from the parameters above, and says which.

## 9. Not done, and needed before any of this is built on

- **Read at the source** everything still marked *search*. Tried on 2026-10-11 and **not** read:
  Mifflin–St Jeor's coefficients (the PubMed page returned no abstract); the ACSM 2026 paper (the
  journal page asks for payment — only ACSM's summary was read); MEDAS's 14 items (in the open
  PLoS ONE paper they are an image, not text: Martínez-González et al. 2012, doi
  10.1371/journal.pone.0043134, CC BY); USDA's own licence sentence; the Henry equations.
- **Which authority is the default** — European (EFSA, WHO, a European food table) or U.S. — is
  the owner's to choose, and the app says which either way.
- **The U.S. *Daily Servings by Calorie Level* document** (2025–2030) was not read.
- **Micronutrient reference tables** (DRI, EFSA) were not fetched; nothing here is a table.
- **Food groups.** A vegetarian or vegan check needs each food classified. Ciqual foods carry
  Ciqual's group; BLS and USDA foods carry none. EFSA's FoodEx2 would be the European
  classification to adopt — not examined, and neither table publishes its foods' FoodEx2 codes.
- **Licences** for recipe text, the Compendium's tables, and screening questionnaires.
- **Safety.** No source has been chosen for a lowest sensible energy target, for who should not
  be offered a deficit, or for when to say "ask a clinician". Until one is, the app sets no floor
  and offers no deficit of its own.
- **Not medical advice** has to be said somewhere a person sees it; where and how is undecided.
