# formicaria on iPhone — what it would actually take

The iOS counterpart to [`mobile-design.md`](./mobile-design.md), written 2026-09-02 against the
v0.4.0 tree. **Nothing here is a decision** — it is the survey that has to exist before decisions
get made, and it survived an adversarial pass that refuted eight of its first draft's claims.
Where a statement is *reasoned from documentation* rather than *observed*, it says so inline; the
unknowns are collected at the end with what would settle each.

**The recommendation, up front: do the two Android bug fixes now, on their own merits. Then either
spend ~$6–12 of CI proving the port compiles and boots — agent-free — or do not start.** There is
no third option worth the money, and the evidence below points at **Route C: iOS without the
assistant, or no iOS.**

---

## The three constraints, all settled by the owner (2026-09-02)

| | Ruling | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **No Mac** | A bounded allocation of GitHub `macos-latest` jobs, spent strategically | No interactive debugging. Every question costs a job. **Measured 2026-09-03: 3–8 min, not the 25–45 reasoned here** — see the ladder |
| **No iPhone** | The Simulator is the only device this will ever run on | **The entire Apple bill is deferred.** A Simulator build needs no signing, no profile, no $99/yr, no review, no annual renewal |
| **Repo stays private** | macOS minutes are billed (~$1.50–$3.00/job) | Stated in the tree, not inferred: `.github/workflows/ci.yml:3` — *"this is a private repo, so minutes are billed and GitHub multiplies them"* |

**The no-iPhone ruling is the one that reshapes everything.** Almost all of what makes iOS
expensive — membership, certificates, the 90-day TestFlight treadmill, the annual mandatory-SDK
deadline gating *App Store Connect uploads*, review latency — attaches to **shipping**. Shipping is
not on the table. Proving the port is, and it is cheap.

**And it sets a ceiling that has to be said plainly: this work cannot produce an iOS release.**
`features.md` already carries *"never yet run on macOS or Windows"* as an accepted desktop gap.
iOS would be strictly worse — an App Store app, for users the owner cannot observe, on hardware
never tested, with a multi-day review cycle instead of a rebuild. The honest goal is *"the port is
real and we know what it would cost"*. Anything more is a promise nothing here can keep.

**"Make the repo public" is not a CI optimisation and must not be smuggled in as one.** It would
expose `docs/context/`, the supervision datasheet and every internal review. It is a large separate
decision; it happens to make macOS runners free, which is not a reason to take it.

## The cheapest answer already ships, and the iOS case must beat it

**An iPhone can open the real formicaria today, at zero cost.** *Settings → Share with another
device* adds a second listener over TLS; the phone opens the app in Safari, entirely
origin-relative, scoped per vault by `fm_app::Scope`, behind a pairing code and an `HttpOnly`
token. The feature was written with Apple devices in view — `ui/src/lib/SettingsPanel.svelte:764`
names *"Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings"*, and the share ruling
(`decisions.md#vault`, the self-signed-leaf TLS exception) notes mDNS is resolved by both iOS and
Android. Over it an iPhone gets the whole notebook: editing, board, agenda, timeline, search,
whiteboard, capture, blobs.

A **PWA / Add-to-Home-Screen** over that same TLS origin is a further free step — with the
certificate installed it is a secure context, so it gets `getUserMedia`, and it needs no Apple
money, no Xcode and no second shell.

What neither gives: **offline, away from the desk, and a vault that lives on the phone as a git
peer.** Those are precisely what the Android build exists for — *"the app runs on the phone
itself"* — set against the MASTERPLAN's thin-client framing.

**So the whole question reduces to one sentence.** *Is offline, away-from-the-desk editing on an
iPhone worth a CI-only build loop, a forced Xcode session every year, and — if it is ever to reach
a user — $99/yr forever?* Answer that first. Everything below is downstream of it.

## What is already true, and costs nothing (the good news)

Marked **[observed]** where checked in this tree or in pinned crate source, **[documented]** where
reasoned from vendor documentation and *not* verifiable from this machine.

- **[observed] Git is the easiest part, and easier than Android.** `libgit2-sys 0.18.5/build.rs`
  selects `GIT_SECURE_TRANSPORT` for any `target.contains("apple")` and links `framework=Security`.
  That means the iOS system trust store, so the entire Android CA saga — `install_ca_bundle`, the
  concatenated PEM, `GIT_OPT_ADD_SSL_X509_CERT`, the `no-stdio` discovery — is **unnecessary on
  iOS, not merely broken**. `native-git` stays the backend; the vendored-libgit2 exception in
  `deny.toml` already covers an IPA exactly as it covers an APK. *(Risk: Apple deprecated
  SecureTransport and libgit2 has discussed removing `stransport.c`.)*
- **[observed] Every C dependency cross-compiles** to `aarch64-apple-ios`: rusqlite's bundled
  SQLite, libgit2-sys, ring; sha2/infer/flate2/tar/zip are pure Rust. Only vendored OpenSSL is
  wasted — see groundwork item G1.
- **[observed] `config_dir()` already works** — `vaults.rs:284-313` has a
  `#[cfg(not(any(linux, macos, windows)))]` catch-all reading `FM_CONFIG_DIR`, and iOS lands in it.
  **Fragile, not robust:** anyone "tidying" the macOS arm to include iOS would silently reroute the
  phone to `$HOME/Library/Application Support` and break the managed-root model. Worth a comment.
- **[observed] The pinned model sizes are modest.** `agents/models.toml`: `default_mobile =
  lfm2.5-1.2b` at 730,895,168 bytes and `ggml-base.en` at 147,964,211 — ~879 MB on first enable
  with transcription, not the multi-GB laptop pick. Resident cost is **~1.4 GB measured on
  Android**; whether an iPhone tolerates that is **unknown** and is in the unknowns list.
- **[documented] The first-enable screen already satisfies review guideline 4.2.3(ii)** (*"disclose
  the size of the download and prompt users before doing so"*) — that is exactly what shipped on
  2026-09-02. Downloading *weights* is permitted and shipping today (PocketPal AI); downloading
  *executables* is not.
- **[documented] iOS has no Android freeze tax.** The `(async)`-on-every-command rule exists because
  Tauri's `ipc-protocol.js` opens `const canUseCustomProtocol = osName !== 'android'`. iOS is not
  excluded, so it gets async IPC and raw request bodies — and could drop the base64 ingest
  workaround and its 16 MB ceiling. An opportunity, not a task.
- **[documented] The CSP already permits what iOS needs.** `mobile/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json:21`
  lists `fmblob:` alongside `http://fmblob.localhost`, and `blobBase()` derives the origin at
  runtime. iOS uses real `WKURLSchemeHandler` schemes, which the policy already covers.

## Three things that cannot ship as they stand

### 1. The assistant is a subprocess architecture, and iOS forbids processes

**[documented]** Review guideline 2.5.2 forbids downloading or executing code; below the policy the
OS agrees absolutely — `fork()`, `posix_spawn()`, `NSTask`, `popen()` all fail, confirmed by Apple
DTS. **[observed]** Our stack assumes a child at every layer: `mobile/src-tauri/src/agent.rs:442`
(llama-server) and `:501` (whisper); `fm-agent/src/launch.rs:36` spawns; `watchdog.rs:202` kills;
`ci/android-stage-runtime.sh` stages the binary into `jniLibs` under a `lib*.so` name so Android's
W^X will exec it. `agent.rs` contains **zero `cfg` gates**, so an iOS build compiles, installs,
launches, and fails at runtime with a message naming `/proc/self/maps` — on a platform with no
`/proc`.

> **This already contradicts a standing ruling, and the reversal was never written.** The
> iOS-eventually ruling (`decisions.md#track-m`, 2026-07-19) states the consequence outright: *"no
> design may assume an executable subprocess on device, on either platform."* The shipped Android
> agent does exactly that, and no reversal exists. Per `CLAUDE.md`, **that dated reversal must be
> written first**, with a `> SUPERSEDED` banner on the 2026-07-19 entry — and the choice between
> Routes A, B and C *is* that reversal's content.

| | **A — link llama.cpp** | **B — Apple Foundation Models** | **C — no assistant on iOS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| How | `llama-cpp-2` linked in-process | Swift plugin over the iOS 26 model | `--no-default-features`; it already compiles out |
| Keeps | one shared Rust core, identical answers | nothing — a second backend behind `OpenAiStep` | the one-command-surface property, untouched |
| Costs | `llama-cpp-sys-2`'s `build.rs` has **no iOS branch**; days of CMake-to-iOS work, on a Mac | iPhone 15 Pro+ only, no control over the model, **two inference paths to keep answering alike** — a fork of the kind the one-command-surface ruling exists to prevent | the assistant is desktop+Android only, said plainly |

**Choose C.** Neither A nor B can be *evaluated* in a Simulator — no jetsam pressure, no on-device
Metal, no honest tokens/sec — so picking between them from a screenshot is guessing. And B's
"ships sooner and free" prices the build, not the decade.

**[documented] Audio would not use whisper.cpp either:** `whisper-rs` was archived 2025-07-30 with
its iOS build issue unresolved, while iOS 26's `SpeechAnalyzer` does on-device long-form
transcription with no download. That is a *fourth* route and a fourth seam question — more reason
for C.

### 2. The body merge shells `git merge-file` — a live Android bug, escalating since July

**[observed]** `crates/fm-core/src/merge.rs:273` runs `Command::new("git").arg("merge-file")`, with
no `cfg`. On the phone: `git_native.rs:884` calls it with `?` inside the conflict loop that
`repo.merge()` opened at `:860`, and `dispatch.rs:1422` propagates the error straight to the UI.
`known-issues.md:981` already records, verified on the owner's device, that `command -v git` finds
nothing there.

**When it fires.** libgit2 auto-merges what it can; `merged_text` is reached only for paths left
conflicted in the index. But `updated:` is rewritten on every save, so *any* two concurrent edits
to one note collide there — and if both sides also touched the body, the prose goes to `text_3way`
and the pull dies. **That is the ordinary two-device case the feature exists for**, and the
two-phones / phone-plus-desktop user hits it first.

**The consequence is worse than "mid-merge".** `git_native.rs:222-227` says a standing `MERGE_HEAD`
*"freezes the vault permanently"*, and `:582` shows `commit_all` refusing while one exists. So the
phone can neither commit nor push until the user finds the Conflicts surface and keeps a side by
hand — guided by an error naming a binary that cannot exist on that device.

**What is new, precisely.** `decisions.md` (2026-07-19) already recorded the *gap* — *"no engine
exists that a phone can call, under any backend"*. What changed is that the native pull shipped on
2026-07-24, so it is no longer a missing feature but **an erroring pull**. `known-issues.md` does
not carry it.

**And the fix is smaller than the first draft claimed — but less decided.** The **differential
harness already exists and already runs**: `crates/fm-core/tests/merge_differential.rs`, 217 lines,
no feature gate, inside `pixi run ci`. What does *not* exist is the engine, and the candidate this
document first named is refuted: `mobile-design.md:158` is headed *"⛔ refuted 2026-07-18 — that
function does not exist"*, and `:177` marks what follows as superseded reasoning. Three live
candidates remain — a direct `libgit2-sys` `git_merge_file` FFI (**cheaper now**: `libgit2-sys` is
already an optional `fm-core` dependency), `gix-merge`, or a pure-Rust crate (`diffy` is rejected
by name). **Picking one is its own dated `decisions.md` entry under `#git`, written before the work
is scheduled.**

### 3. Nothing runs in the background

**[observed]** `ci/android-inject-service.sh` injects a foreground service whose header records
why: without it *"the ~150 MB model download stalls with DNS failures — seen on-device"*.
**[documented]** iOS gives a suspended app ~30 s, then nothing; the sanctioned routes are a
background `URLSession`, `BGAppRefreshTask`, and `BGProcessingTask` (idle-only, no guarantee).

Under Route C the download problem disappears with the assistant. What remains: **auto-push cannot
be assumed to happen backgrounded.** Frame sync as *"when you open the app"* — honest, and what iOS
offers. libgit2 push is not resumable, so being killed mid-transfer must never leave the repo
inconsistent.

## The CI ladder — a spend plan, not a task list

Each rung is one or two `workflow_dispatch` jobs on `macos-latest`. The **estimate was ~$1.50–$3.00
and 25–45 min**; three runs later the **measured** figures are **2.8–7.7 min wall clock**, i.e.
roughly **$0.20–$0.60 a job** at macOS's 10× multiplier. The estimate was pessimistic by about 5×,
which is worth knowing before deciding a rung is too expensive to try. Time went: `tauri ios build`
360s, pixi restore 47s, `tauri ios init` (including a Homebrew `xcodegen` install) 29s, frontend 12s
— so **the compile is essentially the whole bill**, which is what makes the cache settings below
matter more than anything else on this page.

**Caching, corrected 2026-09-03 after the owner asked whether we cache what is cacheable.** Two
`Swatinem/rust-cache` defaults were costing real money. `cache-on-failure` is **false** by default,
so a failing job saves nothing — verified by the absence of any `Post Run Swatinem/rust-cache` step
in either failed rung-2 run, while rung 1's successful run has one. Every retry was therefore
recompiling vendored OpenSSL, libgit2 and SQLite from scratch, *and* discarding the result again.
For a rung expected to fail a few times while the harness is debugged that is exactly backwards: the
compile is valid whether or not the simulator later booted. And `add-job-id-key` is **true** by
default, so rung 1 and rung 2 each kept a private cache despite rung 1 building precisely the C that
rung 2 needs; both now use `shared-key: ios`. Nothing else is worth caching — init is 29s and the
frontend 12s, and `gen/apple` is deliberately regenerated.

and **each is a stop-and-decide point**. Batch aggressively: there is no second look. Rungs 1–4

### Rung 2 — written 2026-09-03, **not fired**

The script is `ci/ios-smoke.sh` (`pixi run ios-smoke`), the job is `ios.yml`'s `rung2`, and the
workflow now takes a `rung` input so **one dispatch spends one job**. It asserts what
`android-smoke.sh` asserts, in the same order and against the same `vaults ready` line: two
consecutive cold launches, each painted (`vips deviate` over a `simctl io screenshot`) and each
reporting the store open on the pty. It also records what `app_data_dir()` resolved to, which is the
input G5 has been waiting for.

**Four things were settled by reading the Tauri v2.11.4 CLI source before writing a line of it**, and
each would have cost a job to discover:

- **A bare `xcodebuild` cannot build this project.** The generated "Build Rust Code" phase runs
  `tauri ios xcode-script`, which unconditionally calls `read_options()` — it reads a
  `<bundle-id>-server-addr` file from the temp dir and opens a WebSocket to a server that exists
  only inside a *running* `tauri ios build`/`dev`, and `panic!`s when the file is missing. So the
  build goes through the CLI, and the "just drive xcodebuild directly" plan is dead.
- **`--no-sign` is required, not tidiness.** The CLI runs `xcodebuild archive` for the *simulator*
  target on the same path as a device build; only `--no-sign` injects `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO`, and
  a headless runner has no keychain, certificate or team. (It skips `-exportArchive` for simulator
  targets, so no IPA and no provisioning profile are involved.)
- **tauri#15066 is not Tauri's bug and it has a documented fix.** Root cause is
  `actions/runner-images#13135`: Xcode 26 puts `MetalToolchain` ahead of `XcodeDefault` in the
  toolchain search order, and MetalToolchain ships no Swift back-deployment libraries. The
  maintainers' fix is `TOOLCHAINS=com.apple.dt.toolchain.XcodeDefault`, which Tauri's own
  `xcodebuild` calls cannot inject — so the script exports it. Applied defensively: every report is
  against the device SDK and nothing found says the Simulator SDK is immune.
- **`--target aarch64-sim`** is one of exactly three accepted values, and the built app lands at
  `gen/apple/build/arm64-sim/<productName>.app`. The script still discovers it by glob, along with
  the bundle id (`plutil` on the built `Info.plist`) and the device — everything it needs is read at
  runtime, because it is written blind and must survive the next `tauri ios init`.

Cargo features are left at their defaults: `build.rs`'s `agent_shell` gates `mod agent`, so no iOS
build runs an assistant whatever the invocation, and the script depends on no flag nobody here can
verify. It does **not** prune the graph — `fm-agent`, `fm-agent-run` and their `download` tail
(`ureq` → `rustls` → `ring`) still compile for `aarch64-apple-ios-sim`, for the first time anywhere,
inside the job. `check-cross` covers `fm-agent` only by design (`ring`'s C build script needs an
Apple SDK), so **a failure inside `ring` is a known-unmeasured edge, not a mystery.**

**An adversarial review of the whole change set caught four things that would each have wasted the
job**, before a single minute was spent. Recorded because three of them are shaped like the mistakes
this project keeps making:

- **The smoke step ran in the wrong pixi environment.** `pixi run ios-smoke` takes `default`, whose
  sysroot has *zero* `apple-ios` entries (measured); the iOS `rust-std` packages live in
  `[feature.cross]`. Rung 1 got this right on every Rust step (`pixi run -e cross …`) and rung 2
  simply dropped the `-e cross`.
- **`tauri ios init` installs *all three* Apple triples, fatally.** `cargo-mobile2`'s `project::gen()`
  calls `Target::install_all()` — `aarch64-apple-ios`, `x86_64-apple-ios`, `aarch64-apple-ios-sim` —
  and `?`s on the first failure; `--target aarch64-sim` narrows the *build*, not the init. Only two
  were pinned, so `ci/bin/rustup` would have refused the third and taken init down. **Rung 1 passed
  only because no shim was on PATH then and the runner's own rustup answered** — i.e. the shim, added
  in this very change set as a safety measure, is what would have broken it. `x86_64-apple-ios` is
  now pinned; verified by running the exact call for all three.
- **The blank-screen check was vacuous.** `android-smoke.sh` waits for `dumpsys window` to name our
  app before measuring a pixel. iOS has no `dumpsys`, and the first screenshot after `simctl launch`
  is the **home screen** — wallpaper and icons, deviation far above any blank-screen floor — so the
  check passed at t+0 and printed "painted". *A wrong answer bought with a billed job is worse than a
  failure.* Fixed by ordering: `vaults ready` on the pty is the gate this platform does have, and the
  pixel check runs after it (a gray screen still logs ready and still fails). The home screen is kept
  as a screenshot and its deviation written to `stats.txt` so the numbers can be read against
  something.
- **The UDID parse broke on `iPhone SE (3rd generation)`** — "second parenthesised group" is the UDID
  only for names without brackets, and that device is in the default set. It would have handed
  `simctl bootstatus` the string `3rd generation`, *after* the 25-45 minute build was paid for. Both
  it and the device-type parse now match by identifier shape; verified against captured `simctl`
  output.

### Rung 2, first firing — **failed at `tauri ios init`, and not on iOS** (run 91349022694)

One job spent. It never reached a compile, a simulator or a screenshot.

```
Downloading @tauri-apps/cli-darwin-x64@2.11.4          <- on an arm64 runner
Info [cargo_mobile2::apple::deps] Installing `xcodegen` with brew...
error: Cannot install under Rosetta 2 in ARM default prefix (/opt/homebrew)!
```

**The cause was a pixi environment skew, not Apple.** Rung 1 ran every pnpm step in the `default`
environment and passed; rung 2 ran them in `cross`, because that is the only environment carrying
the iOS `rust-std` packages. On `osx-arm64` the two had resolved **different package managers** —
pnpm 11.13.1 in `default`, 12.2.1 in `cross` — and `cross`'s re-resolved `@tauri-apps/cli` to the
**x86_64** binary. The Tauri CLI ran under Rosetta 2, and the first thing it does is shell out to
Homebrew, which refuses to install into an ARM prefix from an emulated process. Full reasoning:
`decisions.md`, *the JS toolchain is pinned across every pixi environment*.

Fixed three ways, cheapest first: `nodejs`/`pnpm` pinned in `[dependencies]` so no environment can
drift; rung 2's frontend step moved into `cross` so one environment does all of it; and
`ci/ios-smoke.sh` now compares the resolved CLI's architecture against `uname -m` **before** the
build, so a recurrence is one line instead of a Homebrew message 40 seconds in. A `ci/checks.sh`
guard compares the environments.

### Rung 2, second firing — **the Rosetta fix held; it now fails at the link** (run 91351661719)

Much further: `tauri ios init` succeeded, the Rust side compiled, and `xcodebuild archive` reached
the **linker**. It is *not* tauri#15066 — the `TOOLCHAINS` workaround held, and no
`-lswiftCompatibility56` appeared. Twelve undefined symbols, all system libraries libgit2 needs:

```
"_inflate" "_inflateInit_" "_inflateEnd" "_inflateReset"
"_deflate" "_deflateInit_" "_deflateEnd" "_deflateReset" "_crc32"   <- zlib
"_iconv" "_iconv_open" "_iconv_close"                               <- libiconv
  referenced from libapp.a(zstream.o, indexer.o, filebuf.o, fs_path.o)
ld: symbol(s) not found for architecture arm64
```

**A `staticlib` cannot carry a system dylib**, and the final link here is Xcode's, not cargo's — so
`libgit2-sys`'s `rustc-link-lib=z`/`=iconv` reach nobody. Android is immune because it builds a
`cdylib` that rustc links. Fixed by `ci/ios-inject-linker-libs.sh`, which adds `- sdk: libz.tbd` and
`- sdk: libiconv.tbd` to the generated `project.yml` and re-runs `xcodegen`; the three tidier
mechanisms were each checked against the pinned CLI source and rejected — see `decisions.md`,
*iOS gets zlib and iconv from the Xcode project*.

Timings, which is also the answer to "are we caching what is cacheable": `tauri ios build` **360s**,
pixi restore 47s, `tauri ios init` (including a Homebrew `xcodegen` install) 29s, frontend 12s.

### Rung 2, third firing — **it builds, installs and launches. It does not speak.** (run 91357302585)

The furthest yet, and the first run where iOS itself is the subject rather than our build config:

```
ios-inject-linker-libs: added libz.tbd + libiconv.tbd to .../project.yml
ios-inject-linker-libs: formicaria-mobile.xcodeproj references both libraries
ios-smoke: built .../build/arm64-sim/formicaria.app      <- the link succeeded
ios-smoke: bundle id dev.formicaria.notes
ios-smoke: device 6F69910C-…                             <- the UDID parser held
ios-smoke: installed
FAIL: launch 1: no 'vaults ready' line in 60s
--- console-1.log ---                                    <- empty
```

**An iOS app of this project exists, links and installs.** That is rung 2's first half answered.
The `simctl launch` process stayed alive the whole 60s (the exit check never fired), so the app was
*running* — it produced nothing on the pty.

**And the harness could not tell us which failure this was**, because the previous fix had made the
log assertion gate the pixel check: no `vaults ready`, therefore no screenshot, therefore no way to
distinguish *"runs but cannot speak"* from *"never painted"* — the exact distinction the job was
bought to make. Both are now polled in one loop, the home-screen baseline keeps the pixel check
honest, and the verdict names which half failed, because they mean opposite things:

| painted | spoke | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | ❌ | the app runs; **the diagnostic channel is wrong** — the `os_log` reversal trigger, *not* a kill |
| ❌ | ✅ | the store opened and the screen did not — the gray-screen signature, on iOS |
| ❌ | ❌ | **the kill criterion.** Record and stop |

The next run also captures `simctl spawn <udid> log stream` from before the launch — the fallback
the kill criterion names, run automatically rather than left as an instruction that would itself
cost a job to act on — plus any crash report. **`os_log` is deliberately not being pre-built**: the
unified-log capture may well show the stderr already, and a C shim that cannot be compiled here is
not worth writing speculatively when the answer costs ~$0.30.

### Rung 2, fourth firing — **the app runs and paints on iOS.** (run 91364602829)

The verdict machinery worked, and it returned the informative half:

```
FAIL: launch 1: **the app painted but never said 'vaults ready'**
  (painted t+1s, deviation 35.408179 vs home 71.743298; launch process still alive)
--- console-1.log (pty) ---        <- byte-empty
--- oslog-1.log (unified log) ---  <- 30s of formicaria[45290] WebKit traffic
```

**Three facts, and the third is the one that matters.** The app painted a screen distinct from the
home screen within a second. The unified log carries thirty seconds of that process's WebKit
activity — resources loading through Tauri's custom scheme handler, `didComplete` each time — so it
was unambiguously alive and its WebView was working. And **neither the pty nor the unified log
contained a single one of our records**, which means the previous rung's "empty pty" was not a fluke
and stderr reaches nobody here.

So an iOS build of formicaria **runs**. What it could not do was speak, which hid the actual
question — *did the store open?* Fixed by giving the logger a second sink it cannot lose: a file in
the app's own container (`decisions.md`, *the iOS log is a file in the app container*), read by the
harness through `simctl get_app_container`. The smoke test now polls three channels and **reports
which one spoke**, so the day stderr starts working we will know rather than assume.

That fix caught a second defect for free: the first draft used `log::set_boxed_logger`, which lives
behind `log`'s `std` feature and would not have compiled. Running the logger in a throwaway crate on
Linux found it; a runner would have found it for ~$0.30.

**Still open after four rung-2 firings:** whether `boot()` completes. Everything above is consistent
with both "the store opened and we could not hear it" and "the store never opened". One more run
answers it, and that is the whole remaining question of rung 2.

**Two things worth keeping from a failed job.** The runner is `macos-26-arm64` with Xcode 26.6, and
`cargo-mobile2` installs `xcodegen` via Homebrew on every `init` — it is not preinstalled, it worked
in rung 1, and it is a network dependency in the critical path. Also: the artifact upload worked,
returning `ios-init.log` and the disk snapshot, so the observation channel this rung depends on is
real even when everything else fails.

**Cost so far: three jobs.** Rung 1 twice (one lost to a `libgit2-sys` defect that was also live on
Windows), rung 2 once. None of the three was lost to iOS being hard; all three were lost to this
project's own build configuration, which is the honest summary to carry into rung 2's second firing.

Three `ci/checks.sh` guards were also found satisfiable without the property holding — the `mod
agent;` gate passed when the declaration was *absent*, and the `build.rs` gate passed with `&&`
flipped to `||` or `==` to `!=`, because it checked three tokens and not the expression joining them.
All are now proven by mutation.

Downstream of rung 1, in order: ~~a diagnostic backend for `install_logger()`~~ **done 2026-09-03,
stderr rather than `os_log`**; ~~`ci/ios-smoke.sh`~~ **done, unrun**; the Info.plist usage strings —

## Still open: where a vault lives, and what "you own your notes" means on iOS

**[documented]** `UIFileSharingEnabled` + `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace` puts the vault under
*On My iPhone* in Files: real loose `.md` the user can browse, hand to another app, and recover
through device backup — the closest iOS gets to files-as-truth, and where Obsidian landed for the
same reason.

**It has a price.** `ColdStart::TrustIndex` (`lib.rs:60-81`) rests on *"a phone has no writer that
preserves mtime — that is a fact about the device."* Exposing the folder to Files and third-party
document providers **falsifies that claim**, and iOS would have to revert to `ColdStart::Rebuild`.
**The two decisions must be made together.** What does not survive either way is "point formicaria
at a folder anywhere" — on iOS that is a one-shot import through the picker. **iCloud Drive is not
an option**: dataless files mean `stat()` succeeds while `read()` materialises over the network.

## What we could not verify from here

Each with what would settle it. **Everything asserted about iOS *runtime* behaviour above is
[documented], not observed** — that distinction is the whole point of this list.

- **Whether an iPhone tolerates ~1.4 GB resident.** Jetsam limits are per-device and undocumented;
  under Route A the model would sit in the app's *own* address space, not a child's. → Route C
  makes it moot; otherwise, hardware.
- **`llama-cpp-sys-2` cross-compiling to iOS** — no report found either way. → A Mac.
- **Tauri's `app_data_dir()` on iOS** — decides both iCloud-backup exposure and Files
  reachability, hence the vault decision. → One Simulator run (rung 2).
- **Whether WKWebView grants `getUserMedia`** on Tauri's custom scheme. → Rung 3.
- **Whether async IPC, the `fmblob:` scheme and the CSP behave as documented.** → Rungs 2–3.
- **libgit2's SecureTransport lifetime** — deprecated by Apple, and libgit2 has discussed removing
  `stransport.c`. → Rung 4 answers today; nothing answers the next five years.
- **Build minutes for this workspace on a 3-core runner** (reasoned band 25–45 min cold). Note the
  runner is 3 CPU / 7 GB / **14 GB disk** and this build compiles vendored OpenSSL, libgit2 and
  SQLite from source — expect the first attempts to fail on disk, not on code.
