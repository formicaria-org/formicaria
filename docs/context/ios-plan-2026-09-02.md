# formicaria on iPhone — what it would actually take

The iOS counterpart to [`mobile-design.md`](./mobile-design.md), written 2026-09-02 against the
v0.4.0 tree. **Nothing here is a decision** — it is the survey that has to exist before decisions
get made, and it survived an adversarial pass that refuted eight of its first draft's claims.
Where a statement is *reasoned from documentation* rather than *observed*, it says so inline; the
unknowns are collected at the end with what would settle each.

**The recommendation, up front: do the two Android bug fixes now, on their own merits. Then either
spend ~$6–12 of CI proving the port compiles and boots — agent-free — or do not start.** There is
no third option worth the money, and the evidence below points at **Route C: iOS without the
assistant, or no iOS.**

---

## The three constraints, all settled by the owner (2026-09-02)

| | Ruling | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **No Mac** | A bounded allocation of GitHub `macos-latest` jobs, spent strategically | No interactive debugging. Every question costs a job and 25–45 min |
| **No iPhone** | The Simulator is the only device this will ever run on | **The entire Apple bill is deferred.** A Simulator build needs no signing, no profile, no $99/yr, no review, no annual renewal |
| **Repo stays private** | macOS minutes are billed (~$1.50–$3.00/job) | Stated in the tree, not inferred: `.github/workflows/ci.yml:3` — *"this is a private repo, so minutes are billed and GitHub multiplies them"* |

**The no-iPhone ruling is the one that reshapes everything.** Almost all of what makes iOS
expensive — membership, certificates, the 90-day TestFlight treadmill, the annual mandatory-SDK
deadline gating *App Store Connect uploads*, review latency — attaches to **shipping**. Shipping is
not on the table. Proving the port is, and it is cheap.

**And it sets a ceiling that has to be said plainly: this work cannot produce an iOS release.**
`features.md` already carries *"never yet run on macOS or Windows"* as an accepted desktop gap.
iOS would be strictly worse — an App Store app, for users the owner cannot observe, on hardware
never tested, with a multi-day review cycle instead of a rebuild. The honest goal is *"the port is
real and we know what it would cost"*. Anything more is a promise nothing here can keep.

**"Make the repo public" is not a CI optimisation and must not be smuggled in as one.** It would
expose `docs/context/`, the supervision datasheet and every internal review. It is a large separate
decision; it happens to make macOS runners free, which is not a reason to take it.

## The cheapest answer already ships, and the iOS case must beat it

**An iPhone can open the real formicaria today, at zero cost.** *Settings → Share with another
device* adds a second listener over TLS; the phone opens the app in Safari, entirely
origin-relative, scoped per vault by `fm_app::Scope`, behind a pairing code and an `HttpOnly`
token. The feature was written with Apple devices in view — `ui/src/lib/SettingsPanel.svelte:764`
names *"Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings"*, and the share ruling
(`decisions.md#vault`, the self-signed-leaf TLS exception) notes mDNS is resolved by both iOS and
Android. Over it an iPhone gets the whole notebook: editing, board, agenda, timeline, search,
whiteboard, capture, blobs.

A **PWA / Add-to-Home-Screen** over that same TLS origin is a further free step — with the
certificate installed it is a secure context, so it gets `getUserMedia`, and it needs no Apple
money, no Xcode and no second shell.

What neither gives: **offline, away from the desk, and a vault that lives on the phone as a git
peer.** Those are precisely what the Android build exists for — *"the app runs on the phone
itself"* — set against the MASTERPLAN's thin-client framing.

**So the whole question reduces to one sentence.** *Is offline, away-from-the-desk editing on an
iPhone worth a CI-only build loop, a forced Xcode session every year, and — if it is ever to reach
a user — $99/yr forever?* Answer that first. Everything below is downstream of it.

## What is already true, and costs nothing (the good news)

Marked **[observed]** where checked in this tree or in pinned crate source, **[documented]** where
reasoned from vendor documentation and *not* verifiable from this machine.

- **[observed] Git is the easiest part, and easier than Android.** `libgit2-sys 0.18.5/build.rs`
  selects `GIT_SECURE_TRANSPORT` for any `target.contains("apple")` and links `framework=Security`.
  That means the iOS system trust store, so the entire Android CA saga — `install_ca_bundle`, the
  concatenated PEM, `GIT_OPT_ADD_SSL_X509_CERT`, the `no-stdio` discovery — is **unnecessary on
  iOS, not merely broken**. `native-git` stays the backend; the vendored-libgit2 exception in
  `deny.toml` already covers an IPA exactly as it covers an APK. *(Risk: Apple deprecated
  SecureTransport and libgit2 has discussed removing `stransport.c`.)*
- **[observed] Every C dependency cross-compiles** to `aarch64-apple-ios`: rusqlite's bundled
  SQLite, libgit2-sys, ring; sha2/infer/flate2/tar/zip are pure Rust. Only vendored OpenSSL is
  wasted — see groundwork item G1.
- **[observed] `config_dir()` already works** — `vaults.rs:284-313` has a
  `#[cfg(not(any(linux, macos, windows)))]` catch-all reading `FM_CONFIG_DIR`, and iOS lands in it.
  **Fragile, not robust:** anyone "tidying" the macOS arm to include iOS would silently reroute the
  phone to `$HOME/Library/Application Support` and break the managed-root model. Worth a comment.
- **[observed] The pinned model sizes are modest.** `agents/models.toml`: `default_mobile =
  lfm2.5-1.2b` at 730,895,168 bytes and `ggml-base.en` at 147,964,211 — ~879 MB on first enable
  with transcription, not the multi-GB laptop pick. Resident cost is **~1.4 GB measured on
  Android**; whether an iPhone tolerates that is **unknown** and is in the unknowns list.
- **[documented] The first-enable screen already satisfies review guideline 4.2.3(ii)** (*"disclose
  the size of the download and prompt users before doing so"*) — that is exactly what shipped on
  2026-09-02. Downloading *weights* is permitted and shipping today (PocketPal AI); downloading
  *executables* is not.
- **[documented] iOS has no Android freeze tax.** The `(async)`-on-every-command rule exists because
  Tauri's `ipc-protocol.js` opens `const canUseCustomProtocol = osName !== 'android'`. iOS is not
  excluded, so it gets async IPC and raw request bodies — and could drop the base64 ingest
  workaround and its 16 MB ceiling. An opportunity, not a task.
- **[documented] The CSP already permits what iOS needs.** `mobile/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json:21`
  lists `fmblob:` alongside `http://fmblob.localhost`, and `blobBase()` derives the origin at
  runtime. iOS uses real `WKURLSchemeHandler` schemes, which the policy already covers.

## Three things that cannot ship as they stand

### 1. The assistant is a subprocess architecture, and iOS forbids processes

**[documented]** Review guideline 2.5.2 forbids downloading or executing code; below the policy the
OS agrees absolutely — `fork()`, `posix_spawn()`, `NSTask`, `popen()` all fail, confirmed by Apple
DTS. **[observed]** Our stack assumes a child at every layer: `mobile/src-tauri/src/agent.rs:442`
(llama-server) and `:501` (whisper); `fm-agent/src/launch.rs:36` spawns; `watchdog.rs:202` kills;
`ci/android-stage-runtime.sh` stages the binary into `jniLibs` under a `lib*.so` name so Android's
W^X will exec it. `agent.rs` contains **zero `cfg` gates**, so an iOS build compiles, installs,
launches, and fails at runtime with a message naming `/proc/self/maps` — on a platform with no
`/proc`.

> **This already contradicts a standing ruling, and the reversal was never written.** The
> iOS-eventually ruling (`decisions.md#track-m`, 2026-07-19) states the consequence outright: *"no
> design may assume an executable subprocess on device, on either platform."* The shipped Android
> agent does exactly that, and no reversal exists. Per `CLAUDE.md`, **that dated reversal must be
> written first**, with a `> SUPERSEDED` banner on the 2026-07-19 entry — and the choice between
> Routes A, B and C *is* that reversal's content.

| | **A — link llama.cpp** | **B — Apple Foundation Models** | **C — no assistant on iOS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| How | `llama-cpp-2` linked in-process | Swift plugin over the iOS 26 model | `--no-default-features`; it already compiles out |
| Keeps | one shared Rust core, identical answers | nothing — a second backend behind `OpenAiStep` | the one-command-surface property, untouched |
| Costs | `llama-cpp-sys-2`'s `build.rs` has **no iOS branch**; days of CMake-to-iOS work, on a Mac | iPhone 15 Pro+ only, no control over the model, **two inference paths to keep answering alike** — a fork of the kind the one-command-surface ruling exists to prevent | the assistant is desktop+Android only, said plainly |

**Choose C.** Neither A nor B can be *evaluated* in a Simulator — no jetsam pressure, no on-device
Metal, no honest tokens/sec — so picking between them from a screenshot is guessing. And B's
"ships sooner and free" prices the build, not the decade.

**[documented] Audio would not use whisper.cpp either:** `whisper-rs` was archived 2025-07-30 with
its iOS build issue unresolved, while iOS 26's `SpeechAnalyzer` does on-device long-form
transcription with no download. That is a *fourth* route and a fourth seam question — more reason
for C.

### 2. The body merge shells `git merge-file` — a live Android bug, escalating since July

**[observed]** `crates/fm-core/src/merge.rs:273` runs `Command::new("git").arg("merge-file")`, with
no `cfg`. On the phone: `git_native.rs:884` calls it with `?` inside the conflict loop that
`repo.merge()` opened at `:860`, and `dispatch.rs:1422` propagates the error straight to the UI.
`known-issues.md:981` already records, verified on the owner's device, that `command -v git` finds
nothing there.

**When it fires.** libgit2 auto-merges what it can; `merged_text` is reached only for paths left
conflicted in the index. But `updated:` is rewritten on every save, so *any* two concurrent edits
to one note collide there — and if both sides also touched the body, the prose goes to `text_3way`
and the pull dies. **That is the ordinary two-device case the feature exists for**, and the
two-phones / phone-plus-desktop user hits it first.

**The consequence is worse than "mid-merge".** `git_native.rs:222-227` says a standing `MERGE_HEAD`
*"freezes the vault permanently"*, and `:582` shows `commit_all` refusing while one exists. So the
phone can neither commit nor push until the user finds the Conflicts surface and keeps a side by
hand — guided by an error naming a binary that cannot exist on that device.

**What is new, precisely.** `decisions.md` (2026-07-19) already recorded the *gap* — *"no engine
exists that a phone can call, under any backend"*. What changed is that the native pull shipped on
2026-07-24, so it is no longer a missing feature but **an erroring pull**. `known-issues.md` does
not carry it.

**And the fix is smaller than the first draft claimed — but less decided.** The **differential
harness already exists and already runs**: `crates/fm-core/tests/merge_differential.rs`, 217 lines,
no feature gate, inside `pixi run ci`. What does *not* exist is the engine, and the candidate this
document first named is refuted: `mobile-design.md:158` is headed *"⛔ refuted 2026-07-18 — that
function does not exist"*, and `:177` marks what follows as superseded reasoning. Three live
candidates remain — a direct `libgit2-sys` `git_merge_file` FFI (**cheaper now**: `libgit2-sys` is
already an optional `fm-core` dependency), `gix-merge`, or a pure-Rust crate (`diffy` is rejected
by name). **Picking one is its own dated `decisions.md` entry under `#git`, written before the work
is scheduled.**

### 3. Nothing runs in the background

**[observed]** `ci/android-inject-service.sh` injects a foreground service whose header records
why: without it *"the ~150 MB model download stalls with DNS failures — seen on-device"*.
**[documented]** iOS gives a suspended app ~30 s, then nothing; the sanctioned routes are a
background `URLSession`, `BGAppRefreshTask`, and `BGProcessingTask` (idle-only, no guarantee).

Under Route C the download problem disappears with the assistant. What remains: **auto-push cannot
be assumed to happen backgrounded.** Frame sync as *"when you open the app"* — honest, and what iOS
offers. libgit2 push is not resumable, so being killed mid-transfer must never leave the repo
inconsistent.

## iOS groundwork — status after the 2026-09-02 session

The first draft called this "free and beneficial regardless". **That was false**, and the
correction stands: five of these have no non-iOS beneficiary. What follows is what was actually
done, including one item that was attempted, measured, and reverted.

| | Work | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **G3** | `rust-std-aarch64-apple-ios` **and** `-ios-sim` in `[feature.cross]`, plus two `check-cross` lines | **Done.** Both triples type-check `fm-agent` on Linux. It earned itself on the first run: `plausible` came back as dead code on iOS, which is precisely G4 missing |
| **G4** | iOS `ResourceMonitor` arm | **Done.** `os_proc_available_memory()`, hand-declared (`libc` does not expose it), through the existing `plausible()` guard. It is a *different question* from every other arm — jetsam headroom for **this process**, not the machine's free memory, which is the only honest number iOS offers |
| **G2** | `cfg`-gate the Android-only shell | **Partly done.** `native_lib_dir` is now `#[cfg(target_os = "android")]`, so an iOS build of the shell is a compile error at the call site instead of a runtime sentence naming `/proc/self/maps`. The parsing half stays `cfg`-free and tested. The `AndroidHost` rename and the Conscrypt paths in `install_ca_bundle` are **not** done: the mobile shell cannot be compiled for iOS on this machine, so those edits would be unverifiable churn against a target that does not build yet |
| **G1** | Narrow the OpenSSL gate away from iOS | **Half done, and the other half was measured and reverted.** `fm-core`'s own direct `libgit2-sys`/`openssl-sys` edges are now excluded on iOS, mirroring the Windows fix exactly. **But iOS still compiles a vendored OpenSSL that SecureTransport never calls**, and it must: `git2`'s `https` feature — which we require — lists `openssl-sys` directly, and `libgit2-sys` declares it under `cfg(unix)`. Dropping `vendored-openssl` to shed it leaves a **bare** `openssl-sys` whose build script hunts for a system OpenSSL — *"Could not find directory of OpenSSL installation"*, the identical failure that broke the Windows release. Verified with `cargo tree --target aarch64-apple-ios`. Shedding it needs an upstream change, not a manifest trick |
| **G5** | Container-relative vault paths | **Deliberately deferred**, against the original plan. It rewrites how `vaults.json` persists paths — shipping data-persistence code for three live platforms — to fix a failure that can only occur when an iOS app *updates*, which requires a distribution channel this project has explicitly not built. Doing persistence surgery before knowing whether iOS compiles is the wrong order. Revisit if rung 1 goes green |

**Two things fell out that were not iOS work at all.** `parse_proc` — the Linux/**Android** arm —
was not going through `plausible()`, so the shipping phone bypassed the bounds guard and did an
unchecked `* 1024` on its `MemAvailable` reading. Four of four arms now share it. And
`ci/checks.sh` gained *"every hand-written vcs arm reaches both backends"*, which is the guard that
would have caught `commit_all_as`.

## The CI ladder — a spend plan, not a task list

Each rung is one or two `workflow_dispatch` jobs on `macos-latest`, **~$1.50–$3.00 and 25–45 min**,
and **each is a stop-and-decide point**. Batch aggressively: there is no second look. Rungs 1–4
need no Apple account, no signing and no money beyond CI. **There is no rung 5** — a device install
would cost $99/yr to produce an artifact nobody can run.

| Rung | One job answers | **Kill criterion** |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Does it generate and compile?** `tauri ios init` on the runner; build `fm-core --features native-git` for **`aarch64-apple-ios-sim`** (`macos-latest` is Apple silicon, so that is the Simulator's triple) | Whether the C wall is real, and whether tauri#15066's `-lswiftCompatibility56` break bites us | **Two jobs spent without a compile, and neither documented workaround takes → stop.** Record "not until upstream moves" and close the question |
| **2. Does it boot?** `xcrun simctl` boot/install/launch; `simctl io screenshot`; `log stream` as an artifact | Does the WebView render; does the vault initialise; what `app_data_dir()` returns | Blank screen with no diagnosis after the `os_log` backend exists → stop |
| **3. Does the phone-shaped UI work?** Screenshots at several sizes; drive a note create/edit | Safe-area insets without the Kotlin bridge, `100dvh`, the Excalidraw chunk, the `fmblob:` scheme | Layout is broken in ways nothing on Linux can reproduce → stop; that is the no-Mac ceiling arriving |
| **4. Does git work?** Clone + commit + push against a scratch remote, from the Simulator | **The SecureTransport claim** — the single largest "should be fine" here. If HTTPS git fails, the port loses its point | It fails → stop. The CA problem is back and worse than Android's |

**`gen/apple` is regenerated, never committed.** `.gitignore:44-48` forbids it in terms —
*"it embeds absolute paths, so committing it would be committing this machine"*. Follow the
existing pattern: an idempotent `ci/ios-inject-*.sh` that re-applies our edits after codegen,
exactly as `ci/android-inject-service.sh` does.

Downstream of rung 1, in order: an `os_log` backend for `install_logger()` (**before rung 2**, or
rung 2 buys a screenshot and no diagnosis); the Info.plist usage strings —
`NSCameraUsageDescription`, `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`, `NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription`, whose
absence is a **hard TCC crash**, not a soft denial (**before rung 3**); `ci/ios-smoke.sh` as the
analogue of `android-smoke.sh` — written as a script, not YAML, so it survives.

## Still open: where a vault lives, and what "you own your notes" means on iOS

**[documented]** `UIFileSharingEnabled` + `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace` puts the vault under
*On My iPhone* in Files: real loose `.md` the user can browse, hand to another app, and recover
through device backup — the closest iOS gets to files-as-truth, and where Obsidian landed for the
same reason.

**It has a price.** `ColdStart::TrustIndex` (`lib.rs:60-81`) rests on *"a phone has no writer that
preserves mtime — that is a fact about the device."* Exposing the folder to Files and third-party
document providers **falsifies that claim**, and iOS would have to revert to `ColdStart::Rebuild`.
**The two decisions must be made together.** What does not survive either way is "point formicaria
at a folder anywhere" — on iOS that is a one-shot import through the picker. **iCloud Drive is not
an option**: dataless files mean `stat()` succeeds while `read()` materialises over the network.

## Scope this document does not yet cost

Named so nobody mistakes the ladder for the whole job:

- **UI/UX on iOS**: the edge-swipe back gesture against the board's card drag and the calendar's
  week swipe; the absent hardware back button that `NotePanel`'s full-screen exit binds to
  `popstate`; WKWebView keyboard behaviour over the editor's `<textarea>`; Safari `dvh` semantics.
- **The whiteboard**: a lazily-imported ~744 KB-gz React chunk driving a canvas, with
  `known-issues.md` still listing *"canvas-in-embed … unverified in headless"* — plus pencil/palm
  and two-finger pan.
- **PDFs and the papers track**: `open_external` is an **unimplemented stub** (`lib.rs:162` returns
  *"needs the platform opener, which this build does not have yet"*), so *"the phone opens it
  externally"* is a ruling with no executor. iOS is the one mobile platform whose WebView renders
  PDFs natively — a chance to fix the promise rather than re-inherit it, and `papers-plan.md:69`
  already warns the closest analogue is broken on modern iOS.
- **Migration**: how an existing vault reaches the iPhone (`clone_vault` from the remote, untested
  on iOS, landing wherever the vault-visibility decision puts it).
- **Submission mechanics** beyond 2.5.2/4.2.3 — export-compliance self-classification
  (`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`; the app does TLS git) and guideline 1.2 for a shared vault with
  a discussion-shaped feed. Only relevant if shipping is ever attempted.

## What we could not verify from here

Each with what would settle it. **Everything asserted about iOS *runtime* behaviour above is
[documented], not observed** — that distinction is the whole point of this list.

- **Whether an iPhone tolerates ~1.4 GB resident.** Jetsam limits are per-device and undocumented;
  under Route A the model would sit in the app's *own* address space, not a child's. → Route C
  makes it moot; otherwise, hardware.
- **`llama-cpp-sys-2` cross-compiling to iOS** — no report found either way. → A Mac.
- **Tauri's `app_data_dir()` on iOS** — decides both iCloud-backup exposure and Files
  reachability, hence the vault decision. → One Simulator run (rung 2).
- **Whether WKWebView grants `getUserMedia`** on Tauri's custom scheme. → Rung 3.
- **Whether async IPC, the `fmblob:` scheme and the CSP behave as documented.** → Rungs 2–3.
- **libgit2's SecureTransport lifetime** — deprecated by Apple, and libgit2 has discussed removing
  `stransport.c`. → Rung 4 answers today; nothing answers the next five years.
- **Build minutes for this workspace on a 3-core runner** (reasoned band 25–45 min cold). Note the
  runner is 3 CPU / 7 GB / **14 GB disk** and this build compiles vendored OpenSSL, libgit2 and
  SQLite from source — expect the first attempts to fail on disk, not on code.
