# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is doing their best with less context
than you have — because on a project this size, they usually are.

## What is expected

- **Discuss the work, not the person.** "This breaks on a CRLF checkout" is useful. "Did you even
  test this" is the same information with the useful part removed.
- **Take a correction plainly.** Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of working in public.
  Nobody needs an apology; they need the fix.
- **Say what you actually know.** "I have not tried this on Windows" is worth more than a confident
  guess, and this project has paid for the difference more than once.
- **Respect a no.** Several things are settled and listed in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Arguing a settled
  point once, with a new argument, is fine. Arguing it repeatedly is not.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form; sexualized language or imagery; insults, demeaning comments, or personal
attacks; publishing anyone's private information without permission; sustained disruption; and any
conduct that would reasonably be understood as making this a worse place for someone to
participate because of who they are.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and any other space that
represents the project.

## Reporting

This is a single-maintainer project, so the maintainer is who you report to: open a private report
through **Security → Advisories** on this repository, which is a channel that does not put the
report in public. Say what happened and where. Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's
identity is not shared.

If the maintainer is the problem, there is no higher authority here — that is an honest limit of a
project this size, and the right answer may simply be to walk away. It is not a failing on your
part.

## Consequences

Depending on severity: a private word, an edit or deletion of the content, or a block from the
repository. The maintainer decides, and will say which and why to the person concerned.

## Attribution

rewritten to say what a one-maintainer project can actually promise rather than to describe an
enforcement structure that does not exist here.
